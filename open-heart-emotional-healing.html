Open-Heart Coaching for Emotional Healing

Understanding the “Glass Walls” Within

Most of us have heard of the “glass ceiling”—an unseen barrier that limits advancement. But what about the invisible barriers within us?

At Heart to Heart, we call these “glass walls.” They represent beliefs, fears, and protective patterns that can make it difficult to trust, connect with others, or move forward. You may see the life and relationships you long for, yet feel that something keeps getting in the way.

How these walls develop

Painful experiences in childhood or adulthood can influence what we believe about ourselves, other people, and God. Rejection, neglect, criticism, betrayal, or abuse may lead us to develop ways of protecting ourselves from further hurt.

Those responses may once have helped us cope. Over time, however, they can become so familiar that we no longer recognize them as patterns—or notice how they affect our lives.

You might find yourself withdrawing from others, struggling to express your needs, expecting rejection, avoiding conflict, or staying constantly on guard. Rather than judging these responses, we can approach them with curiosity: What have I experienced, and what might still need care?

When connection feels difficult

If love and care were absent, inconsistent, or unsafe in your early relationships, closeness may feel complicated. You may long for connection while also fearing disappointment, rejection, or abandonment.

Some people use the phrase “orphan wound” to describe a deep sense of being alone, unwanted, or unsupported. These feelings can also influence how a person experiences their relationship with God—for example, finding it difficult to trust His care even while believing in His love.

Recognizing these struggles is not a reason for shame. It is an opportunity to understand your experiences with compassion.

What Is Open-Heart Coaching?

Open-Heart Coaching offers faith-centered support as you explore the emotional aftermath of hurtful experiences and take meaningful steps toward healing.

Sometimes we tell ourselves, “The past is the past,” while familiar feelings and relationship patterns continue to affect us. Coaching creates room to explore those connections without pressure to revisit everything at once.

Through thoughtful questions, reflection, and practical tools, you can begin to:

  • Recognize beliefs and patterns that may be keeping you feeling stuck.
  • Understand your feelings and needs with greater compassion.
  • Strengthen boundaries and practice healthier communication.
  • Rebuild self-trust and consider different ways of responding.
  • Explore your relationship with God and bring your questions and pain into prayer.
  • Take manageable steps forward at a pace that respects your readiness.

Why “Open-Heart”?

The name reflects a willingness to gently acknowledge places in your heart that have been hurt, hidden, or guarded. It is a metaphor for honest reflection and personal growth.

Exploring painful experiences can feel vulnerable. Your coaching relationship should offer a respectful, nonjudgmental setting where you can choose what to share, ask questions, and pause when you need to. Healing does not require forcing yourself through overwhelming emotions.

Faith and the healing journey

At Heart to Heart, we believe Jesus is the Great Healer. Prayer, biblical reflection, and a growing relationship with God are central to Sherée’s approach. Her desire is to walk alongside you with encouragement as you seek understanding, healing, and healthier relationships.

Faith and professional mental-health care can work together. Coaching offers support for reflection and growth; licensed mental-health professionals provide assessment and treatment for trauma-related and other psychological conditions.

Moving forward with hope

Your experiences deserve to be acknowledged, but they do not have to define every chapter ahead. Understanding your “glass walls” can help you recognize where you have choices and begin practicing new ways of relating to yourself and others.

You do not have to have everything figured out to begin. There is room for questions, support, faith, and hope as you take your next step.
