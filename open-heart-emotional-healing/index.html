Open-Heart Coaching for Emotional Healing

Understanding the “Glass Walls” Within

Most of us have heard of the “Glass Ceiling". Definition: an unofficially acknowledged barrier to advancement in a profession from rising beyond a certain level in a hierarchy.

But what about “Glass Walls”? These are the personal barriers that keep us stuck—preventing us from moving forward in life, forming deep and meaningful relationships, emotionally bonding with the closest people in our lives, achieving our dreams, or experiencing true joy. In fact, these walls can even stand between us and a deeper intimate relationship with our Heavenly Father. Not on His part but ours.

We all have these walls in some form, often built from past wounds and painful experiences. The challenge is that they can feel so normal, we may not even recognize they exist. Even if we sense their presence, we usually don’t understand how to get rid of them. These Glass Walls can manifest in many ways—depression, strained relationships, procrastination, fear of failure or success, avoidance, negativity, paralysis, anxiety, self-sabotage, anger, illness, loneliness, bitterness—the list goes on.

These are not the reasons, but these are the outcomes.

The good news? These walls can be broken. With awareness, healing, and faith, we can tear them down and step into the freedom, connection, and purpose we were meant to live.

When connection feels difficult - Orphan Wound

Why do so many people struggle to experience real love—the kind of love God speaks about so often? The truth is, this is a challenge for many, both inside and outside the church. There are many reasons for this, but one of the most significant lies in our early relationships—especially with our fathers.

A father’s role in a child’s life is uniquely powerful. It is through this relationship that we first form our sense of identity, and ideally, it should reflect the way our Heavenly Father loves and interacts with us. But when a father is absent, emotionally distant, abusive, or rejecting, it creates a deep void—a wound that shapes the way we see ourselves, others, and even God.

This void becomes a mindset, something we call the "Orphan Wound." At its core, this wound is about trust—or rather, the struggle to trust in love, including God's love.

Many of us know in our minds that God loves us, but deep down, our hearts wrestle with believing it. This struggle affects our ability to fully embrace the deep intimacy God desires to have with us. While salvation is secure, healing is necessary for us to truly walk in the fullness of God’s promises. Without it, we may find ourselves living in survival mode rather than thriving in the abundance of His love.

What Is Open-Heart Coaching?

Open-Heart Coaching offers faith-centered support as you explore the emotional aftermath of hurtful experiences and take meaningful steps toward healing.

Sometimes we tell ourselves, “The past is the past,” while familiar feelings and relationship patterns continue to affect us. Coaching creates room to explore those connections without pressure to revisit everything at once.

Through thoughtful questions, reflection, and practical tools, you can begin to:

  • Recognize beliefs and patterns that may be keeping you feeling stuck.
  • Understand your feelings and needs with greater compassion.
  • Strengthen boundaries and practice healthier communication.
  • Rebuild self-trust and consider different ways of responding.
  • Explore your relationship with God and bring your questions and pain into prayer.
  • Take manageable steps forward at a pace that respects your readiness.

Why “Open-Heart”?

The name reflects a willingness to gently acknowledge places in your heart that have been hurt, hidden, or guarded. It is a metaphor for honest reflection and personal growth.

Exploring painful experiences can feel vulnerable. Your coaching relationship should offer a respectful, nonjudgmental setting where you can choose what to share, ask questions, and pause when you need to. Healing does not require forcing yourself through overwhelming emotions.

Faith and the healing journey

At Heart to Heart, we believe Jesus is the Great Healer. Prayer, biblical reflection, and a growing relationship with God are central to Sherée’s approach. Her desire is to walk alongside you with encouragement as you seek understanding, healing, and healthier relationships.

Faith and professional mental-health care can work together. Coaching offers support for reflection and growth; licensed mental-health professionals provide assessment and treatment for trauma-related and other psychological conditions.

Moving forward with hope

Your experiences deserve to be acknowledged, but they do not have to define every chapter ahead. Understanding your “glass walls” can help you recognize where you have choices and begin practicing new ways of relating to yourself and others.

You do not have to have everything figured out to begin. There is room for questions, support, faith, and hope as you take your next step.
